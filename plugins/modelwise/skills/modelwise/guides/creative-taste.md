# Creative taste by industry: what award-level work looks like

Craft (`guides/art-direction.md`) makes an image competent. Taste makes it distinctive. Bland work is a mood plus a tagline. Award-level work is one specific idea plus one visible craft decision in the frame: something physically true, odd or exact that a stranger would remember.

**How to use this guide** (step 7, three territories):
1. Find the industry below and read its taste directions.
2. Give each territory a different direction. Draw on another industry's direction only when that creates a deliberate contrast that still fits the brand.
3. Translate the direction's craft recipe into the prompt: lens, light, grade, casting, set, type and colour. Never write "cinematic, high quality" in place of a recipe.
4. **Name one signature craft decision per route**, the thing that makes it unmistakable. Examples: "every prop is printed paper", "shot through the product's own camera", "the headline is the only colour in the frame".
5. Check the route against the industry's **bland tells**. If it uses one, change the idea, not the adjectives.

**Reference brands and campaigns are for your thinking, never for the prompt.** Don't name brands, campaigns, agencies or living photographers in an image prompt, and don't copy a campaign's idea. Describe the craft instead: light, lens, material, era, movement. Award references were checked in October 2026; those marked (unverified) are from general knowledge.

Sources: Cannes Lions 2025 Grand Prix lists (creativereview.co.uk, thedrum.com, campaignme.com), D&AD 2026 Black Pencils (adweek.com, dandad.org), Effie India 2025 (exchange4media.com, afaqs.com), Campaign India and Storyboard18 on Indian work, D&AD's behind-the-work feature on Coinbase "Your Way Out", MediaPost best-in-auto, The Impression's top fashion campaigns of 2025.

**Patterns across every industry:**
- **A product or behaviour change beats a campaign layer.** AXA "Three Words" added domestic-violence cover to its policies; it won Cannes Titanium and Effectiveness Grand Prix and a D&AD Black Pencil. FCB India's "Lucky Yatra" for Indian Railways turned tickets into lottery tickets; it won a PR Grand Prix.
- **Craft is the proof.** Telstra made 26 stop-motion wildlife films (Film Craft Grand Prix). Coinbase "Your Way Out" built a game world in live action: printed suits, pixel-scaled sets, shadowless light (D&AD Black Pencils).
- **Design systems win art direction.** The Ordinary's "The Periodic Fable" by Uncommon took the D&AD 2026 Black Pencil for Art Direction.
- **"Plain portrait on white" now reads as having no point of view,** even in fashion.

## Banking, financial services, insurance
**Directions:**
- **Lived-in proof:** the product rule itself is the creative, rewritten for a real situation (AXA "Three Words"; ACKO "Tailor Test", Cannes Gold in Creative Data).
- **Warm documentary realism:** real families in real homes, never a stock "advisor".
- **Deadpan wit:** a plain-talk consumer truth set against category jargon. Common among digital insurers (unverified).
- **Public-utility ingenuity:** a civic incentive redesigned (Lucky Yatra).

**Craft recipe:**
- Natural window light, 35 or 50mm, a slightly desaturated warm grade.
- Non-model casting with visible age and wear, in lived-in domestic sets.
- Eye level and off-centre; hands with objects (documents, keys, a phone).
- One confident grotesque or a humanist serif, sentence case, left-aligned, generous space, one flat brand colour.

**Voice:**
- A specific rule rewritten: "[Old policy line] now covers [real situation]".
- Second person, no hype adjectives.

**Bland tells:**
- handshake, umbrella and shield icons
- blue-grey gradients
- "your trusted partner", "secure your future"
- a stock couple smiling at sunset
- 40-word headlines

**Avoid:**
- fear of death or accidents
- the patriarch-as-breadwinner cliché
- jargon (premium, rider, sum assured) in the headline

## Fintech and investing apps
**Directions:**
- **Product theatre or world-building:** a practical, built world that dramatises the promise (Coinbase "Your Way Out").
- **Calm editorial minimalism:** big numerals, one bright colour, generous space (unverified).
- **Knowing irreverence:** pokes at old-bank habits with cultural wit; strong in Indian fintech (unverified).
- **Data as art:** the product's own interface shown as graphic motion (unverified).

**Craft recipe:**
- Production design over screens: practical sets, printed textures, a flat or isometric camera, hard single-source or shadowless light.
- Oversized neo-grotesque numerals with mono accents for data, tight tracking.
- One hot accent on near-black or off-white.
- In motion, cut on the beat, and use counters as the hero graphic.

**Voice:**
- Knowing and slightly mischievous: "[Boring thing], but [specific twist]".
- Short imperatives. Never "grow your wealth".

**Bland tells:**
- a neon gradient on navy
- floating coins and phones
- rockets and up-arrows
- glassmorphism cards
- a stock "young professional laughing at a phone"

**Avoid:**
- return promises
- get-rich visuals: supercars, fanned cash (see `guides/regulated.md` for the market's rules)

## B2B SaaS and IT services
**Directions:**
- **Typographic wit:** B2B treated like consumer comedy (GoDaddy "Act Like You Know", Creative B2B Grand Prix).
- **The product made physical:** an absurd, tactile analogy built as a set (unverified).
- **Quietly serious editorial:** a clean grid, data stories, human-scale portraits of real people (unverified).
- **Customer documentary:** the customer's own story, filmed (unverified).

**Craft recipe:**
- Soft overcast key light, a medium-format look, a muted teal-grey grade, real employees in real offices. Or the opposite: fully built colour worlds.
- A grotesque sans with one serif for a human note, very large type.
- A flat 2–3 colour palette with one loud accent.
- Show the interface only in the last third of a film.

**Voice:**
- Dry, self-aware and jargon-mocking: "[Buzzword] is just [plain thing]".
- Reads like a coworker talking, not a sales deck.

**Bland tells:**
- isometric servers and clouds
- handshakes
- "unlock / empower / seamless / transform"
- purple gradients with network nodes
- glowing AI brains

**Avoid:**
- stock people pointing at screens
- unprovable "#1" claims
- AI buzzword soup

## Healthcare, pharma, wellness
**Directions:**
- **Access as craft:** the utility is the creative ("Caption with Intention", which won three Grand Prix; "Nigrum Corpus", Industry Craft).
- **Warm documentary realism:** real patients and natural light; the doctor is not the hero.
- **Playful biology:** simplified anatomy and humour for taboo topics (unverified).
- **Quiet premium wellness:** restraint, tactile close-ups, a serif (unverified).

**Craft recipe:**
- Soft daylight, 35mm, skin texture kept, a palette of muted greens and warm neutrals.
- Casting across ages and body types.
- Hand-drawn organic line in a limited palette.
- A humanist sans or rounded serif at a large, accessible size.
- In film, slow push-ins and natural sound.

**Voice:**
- Calm and plain: "[the symptom you didn't know how to name]", "Ask about [specific]".
- Empathy without cure claims.

**Bland tells:**
- a blue or teal clinical palette
- DNA helixes and heartbeat lines
- smiling white-coat doctors
- silhouettes at sunset
- "live better"

**Avoid:**
- cure or result claims
- shock imagery
- exaggerated before-and-after
- dehumanised patients

## Education and edtech
**Directions** (verified examples are scarce here, so treat these as strong hypotheses):
- **Joyful learner portraits:** mid-effort, not graduation.
- **Character-led surreal humour.**
- **Hand-made typography and collage.**
- **Struggle-to-breakthrough documentary:** easily clichéd; it needs a specific person and a specific detail.

**Craft recipe:**
- Daylight bedrooms and classrooms, handheld 35mm, natural colour with grain.
- Chunky cut-paper or marker illustration.
- A saturated primary palette with one dark ink.
- Rounded geometric sans with a hand-lettered accent; stop-motion or scribble animation.

**Voice:**
- Encouraging, not preachy: "[A 'silly' question] is a good question".
- Name concrete subjects, never "future-ready".

**Bland tells:**
- mortarboards
- glowing brains and light bulbs
- laptops with floating icons
- topper posters with "100% results"

**Avoid:**
- exam shame or fear
- placement guarantees
- parental-pressure imagery

## Consumer D2C, FMCG, beauty
**Directions:**
- **Art-direction-led system:** product science told as a handmade fable ("The Periodic Fable", Black Pencil).
- **Purpose with proof:** Dove (Glass Lion Grand Prix, 2025); "Vaseline Verified" (Social and Creator).
- **Culture-first Indian craft:**
  - Mondelez "Erase Valentine's Day" (Gold, Social and Creator)
  - Ariel "#ShareTheLoad"
  - Maggi's emotional reframe (Grand Effie India 2025)

**Craft recipe:**
- **Beauty:** macro product and skin texture, a mix of hard and soft light, unretouched pores, real-skin casting, a monochrome set.
- **FMCG:** saturated colour, warm tungsten kitchens for emotional work, real family casting.
- **Type:** a distinctive serif or bold grotesque, a hand-lettered claim, clinical sans for ingredients.
- **Motion:** tactile ASMR close-ups.

**Voice:**
- An honest ingredient fact, or a dry self-aware claim.
- In India, Hinglish with a punchline. Humour with substance.

**Bland tells:**
- a pastel gradient with a floating bottle
- a dewy model on white
- "glow / radiance / natural goodness"
- impossible transformations

**Avoid:**
- fairness or skin-lightening framing
- unproven efficacy
- shaming
- a fake "dermatologist recommended"

## Food, beverage, quick-service restaurants
**Directions:**
- **Hyper-local wit:** culture and rituals turned into play (Zepto's "Mithai Wars" with Haldiram's).
- **Cheeky ownership of a product truth:** the product as the hero of a stunt.
- **Fan-culture hacks:** a fan community's own campaign revived (McDonald's regional fan groups).
- **Food as film:** macro, slow motion, ingredient theatre.

**Craft recipe:**
- One hard side light, or backlit steam.
- 100mm macro and a warm grade.
- Real mess (drips, crumbs, condensation), never plastic perfection.
- One saturated brand colour dominating the frame.
- Chunky display type taken from the packaging.
- Sound-led cuts: crunch, sizzle, pour.

**Voice:**
- Playful and short, with an insider cultural reference: "[Ritual or rival], but [our twist]".

**Bland tells:**
- a stock family at a table
- "taste the joy", "fresh and delicious"
- over-styled burgers
- generic sunset barbecues

**Avoid:**
- unhealthy framing aimed at kids
- food that doesn't match the menu

## Real estate
**Directions** (verified examples are scarce here):
- **One family's day:** a lifestyle narrative.
- **Architectural editorial:** the building as sculpture.
- **A love letter to the neighbourhood:** local textures, street food, transport.
- **Market wit:** for example, rent versus EMI.

**Craft recipe:**
- Blue-hour exteriors with warm interior glow, corrected verticals.
- Interiors with window light and practicals, 24mm, lived-in styling.
- A refined serif or a widely tracked sans.
- Stone, brass and deep-green palettes.
- A slow drone glide or a gimbal walk-through.

**Voice:**
- Sensory and concrete: "[Time of day] at [place]", not square footage.

**Bland tells:**
- an over-saturated render with a smiling couple and a giant sunset
- "luxury living", "an address of distinction"
- amenity lists as headlines

**Avoid:**
- misleading renders or distances (in India, these are RERA issues)
- exclusionary cues
- women shown only in kitchens

## Automotive and EV
**Directions:**
- **Product as storyteller:** shot entirely through the car's own cameras (Hyundai "Night Fishing", Entertainment Grand Prix).
- **Calm over specs:** Nissan Ariya's four-hour lo-fi film.
- **Nostalgia meets new:** VW ID. Buzz "Life Half Full".
- **Rebel design manifesto:** Jaguar "Copy Nothing".

**Craft recipe:**
- Long lens (85–200mm), low angle, a tracking shot from a camera car.
- Golden or blue hour, wet asphalt reflections.
- For EVs, minimal empty landscapes and silence as sound design.
- A wide-set sans with a tiny spec line.

**Voice:**
- Short, understated declaratives: "[Common assumption], reversed".

**Bland tells:**
- mountain roads with glowing light trails
- leafy "green future" visuals
- lens flare
- a spec-sheet voice-over
- the family SUV at a lake

**Avoid:**
- unsafe driving
- greenwashing
- unrealistic range claims
- women as decoration

## Luxury and fashion
**Directions:**
- **Auteur photography:** a distinct photographer's point of view drives each season.
- **Landscape as mood:** for example, fog over open countryside.
- **Surreal fashion theatre.**
- **Cultural-star casting.**

**Craft recipe:**
- Film grain, on-camera flash or soft natural light, a medium-format or 35mm film look.
- Unexpected locations, slightly off-centre faces, imperfect casting.
- A muted earth palette or one unusual saturated field.
- High-contrast serif or ultra-light grotesque, a tiny logo, a lot of negative space.
- Long takes with a low edit rate.

**Voice:**
- Almost none: a single word, a name, a cryptic title card.

**Bland tells:**
- a model on plain white with a logo
- gold-and-black glamour
- jewellery bokeh
- "timeless elegance"

**Avoid:**
- discount shouting
- over-retouched skin
- cultural appropriation
- gratuitous logos

## Travel and hospitality
**Directions** (verified examples are scarce here):
- **Candid human moments,** not landscapes.
- **A slow portrait of a place.**
- **Witty local-insider copy.**
- **Warm rituals of journey and pilgrimage.**

**Craft recipe:**
- Golden or blue hour, one small figure in a big frame, 24–35mm.
- A film-like grade with warm skin tones.
- Handwritten postcard notes or a generous serif.
- Drone plus handheld footage with ambient sound.

**Voice:**
- Second person and sensory, with a travel essay's rhythm: "[Local detail], not [tourist cliché]".

**Bland tells:**
- a couple on a beach with cocktails
- infinity pools
- "paradise / escape / discover"
- drone sunsets with lens flare

**Avoid:**
- poverty tourism
- exoticising local people
- over-retouched resorts

## Telecom, energy, utilities
**Directions:**
- **Craft spectacle that proves a promise:** Telstra's stop-motion wildlife.
- **Warmth and humour:** mascot-led work like Vodafone's ZooZoos in India (verify the current state).
- **Energy as human infrastructure:** the people and places it serves.
- **Data as a story.**

**Craft recipe:**
- Stop-motion, miniatures, animatronic texture, saturated colour.
- Or grounded photography: real field workers and real infrastructure, hard daylight, a wide lens.
- A simple sans on a field of brand colour.

**Voice:**
- Warm, with a tangible benefit: "[Place or person] can now [do X]".

**Bland tells:**
- glowing network globes
- wind turbines at sunset
- "connecting the world / powering progress"
- signal-bar icons

**Avoid:**
- greenwashing
- overstated speeds
- ignoring outage pain

## Government, NGOs, social causes
**Directions:**
- **Behaviour-design stunt:** Lucky Yatra.
- **Access as craft:** "Caption with Intention".
- **Truth-telling with data:** "Nigrum Corpus".
- **Change through identity.**

**Craft recipe:**
- Real people and locations, documentary light, subtitle-forward design, minimal CGI.
- Bold sans, or hand-made activist posters in black with one bright colour.
- Raw footage without a swelling score.

**Voice:**
- One stark sentence, then one ask: "[What is happening] is [fact]. [One action]."

**Bland tells:**
- crying-child close-ups
- hands holding a globe
- "together we can"
- walls of shocking statistics

**Avoid:**
- poverty porn
- saviour framing
- fabricated testimonials
- partisanship from official bodies

## Entertainment, gaming, media
**Directions:**
- **Surreal in-world spectacle:** Mercado Livre "Call of Discounts".
- **Fan-culture activation:** "Tracking Bad Bunny".
- **Meta-reflexive fun.**
- **Hyper-local film and streaming humour.**

**Craft recipe:**
- Practical effects and in-camera tricks.
- A stylised grade (neon, or desaturated 90s).
- Custom display titles in poster layouts with genre-coded lettering.
- Fast, rhythmic cuts, or deliberate lo-fi.

**Voice:**
- In-universe and tongue-in-cheek: "[Character] breaks the fourth wall about [trope]".

**Bland tells:**
- key art of floating heads
- exploding cityscapes
- the same blue-orange grade
- AI-slop collage

**Avoid:**
- spoilers
- unlicensed likenesses
- loot boxes aimed at minors
- deceptive gameplay footage

# Craft vocabulary that beats "AI-generic"

Sources: OpenAI's image-generation prompting guide (developers.openai.com/cookbook), Google's Gemini image and video docs (ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs), Midjourney's parameter docs, Paul Arden (It's Not How Good You Are…), John Hegarty ("zig when others zag"), Leica and Adorama on film stocks. Mood notes not confirmed by those sources are standard craft knowledge.

## Bland tells and their counters
| Tell | Write this instead |
|---|---|
| Waxy, poreless skin | "visible pores, fine hair, uneven skin tone, no retouching". OpenAI: ask for pores, wrinkles, fabric wear and imperfections. Drop "perfect, 8K, hyper-detailed". |
| Centred, symmetrical subject | Exact placement: "subject in the lower-left third, empty space upper right", "cropped at the knees", "asymmetric". |
| Even, perfect light | One motivated source: "single hard window light raking from the left, deep shadow on the right, no fill". |
| Teal-orange grade, "cinematic" | A named look: "warm faded pastel like Portra film", "flat overcast daylight, muted greens", "high-key white, no grade". |
| Glossy 3D blobs, gradients | The real material and medium: "matte ceramic", "risograph print with slight misregistration", "paper collage". |
| Stock smile at the camera | A moment: "mid-laugh, looking off-frame at someone unseen", "looking down at the open ledger". Cast specifically: age, build, clothes, job. |
| A generic place | A specific place, era and clutter: "a 1990s tile-shop back room, mismatched stools, a taped handwritten price list". |
| Too clean | Signs it was captured, not rendered: "real photograph", a motion-blurred hand, dust, a slightly tilted horizon. |

## Vocabulary, with the mood each creates
- **Light:**
  - Rembrandt: dramatic, painterly.
  - Split: intense.
  - Butterfly or clamshell: polished beauty.
  - Hard sun or on-camera flash: candid, documentary punch.
  - Practicals (lamps, neon, a screen's glow): lived-in, intimate.
  - Overcast: calm, honest.
  - Golden hour: warm.
  - Rim or backlight: separation, premium.
- **Lens** (models read it as a style cue, not real optics):
  - 24–28mm: immersive, scale.
  - 35–50mm: documentary, natural.
  - 85–135mm: compressed, flattering portrait.
  - Macro: tactile detail.
- **Film looks:**
  - Portra 400: soft, warm skin, pastel.
  - Tri-X black and white: gritty documentary.
  - CineStill 800T: tungsten night with red halation around lights.
  - Ektar 100: saturated, fine grain.
- **Grade:**
  - Lifted blacks: nostalgic.
  - Bleach bypass: gritty.
  - Duotone: graphic, editorial.
  - Colour blocking: pop, poster-like.
- **Composition:**
  - Negative space: premium, copy-ready.
  - Extreme crop: snapshot energy.
  - Foreground framing: depth, intimacy.
  - Low angle: power.
  - High angle: vulnerability.
  - Dutch angle: unease.
  - Scale play (a giant product, a tiny person): wit.
- **Illustration:**
  - Risograph: indie, handmade.
  - Gouache: soft, warm.
  - Linocut: bold heritage.
  - Textured editorial flat: friendly but smart.
  - Collage: surreal wit.
  - Isometric: overused in tech, so a cliché risk.
- **Type-led layouts:**
  - Swiss grid: authority, calm.
  - Editorial: sophistication.
  - Brutalist: raw energy.
  - Kinetic type: momentum (video).
- **Camera moves:**
  - Slow push-in: tension, intimacy.
  - Rack focus: redirects attention without a cut.
  - Whip pan: energy.
  - Crane: a reveal of scale.
  - Handheld: urgency, documentary.
  - Dolly zoom: dread.

## Writing rules that keep specificity
- **Concrete nouns over adjectives:** "three dented brass cups on a zinc counter", not "beautiful, modern, vibrant".
- **Describe a moment, not a mood:** who is doing what, what just happened, and where they're looking.
- **Pair every mood word with an anchor:** "moody" becomes "a single 40W practical, deep shadows, wet pavement".
- **Reference eras, movements and techniques:** "1970s Swiss poster", "1990s catalogue flash photography", "risograph".
- **Never name a living artist or photographer,** and never imitate a recognisable signature. OpenAI refuses living-artist styles, and client work carries the risk. Break the look down into palette, medium, line quality, era, composition and light.
- **Exclusions are generic positive constraints:** "no watermark, no extra text".
- **Reference images:** label each input ("Image 1: product photo") and say what to keep.

## The creative director's taste check (run before recommending a route)
1. Can the idea be said in one sentence, with exactly one surprise?
2. Is the crop or placement a decision, not a default?
3. Is it a real moment, not a pose? Where are they looking, and what just happened?
4. Are the people, props and place specific?
5. Is there one light source you can name, with consistent shadows?
6. Do the surfaces and skin have texture and a flaw or two?
7. Is the palette 2–4 deliberate colours, with no default teal-orange or unmotivated gradient?
8. Would it still work with the logo covered?
9. Would a competitor's brief produce the same image? If yes, kill it and pick another direction.
10. What can be removed? Restraint is a decision.
11. Does it hold up when someone looks for the flaw? Seek criticism, not praise (Arden).
